    Para el profe: how this scene works
    • Accents never block progress. Students can use the accent bar or the typing shortcuts on any Windows keyboard, with no settings to change. Missed accents are pointed out after the reply is accepted.
    • Answers are matched loosely: capitals, punctuation, extra words and missing accents are all fine. Meaning-changing accents (sí/si, tú/tu, años/anos) get a specific note.
    • Common 10-3Y errors get targeted feedback instead of a generic "wrong": soy quince años, estoy de, me llamo es, a bare name or number, a question without ¿, and replies typed in English.
    • After two missed tries, the hint appears on its own. Hints and retries are counted for the session summary.
    • Outcomes practised: A–6.1 a–c (greetings, introductions, name and age), LC–4.1 a (tú vs. usted), LC–1.3 a (people around me), LC–3.2 a (short written sentences).
    • Nothing is saved or sent anywhere. Everything runs in the student's browser.
    • Audio uses one Spanish voice from this computer: checking…. Microsoft Edge usually offers natural-sounding Microsoft voices. If it says “none found”, the audio buttons are hidden and the scene works with text only.
