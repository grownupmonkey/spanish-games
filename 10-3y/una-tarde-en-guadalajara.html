Una tarde en Guadalajara

Abuela Rosa and Lucía will send you messages all afternoon. Listen, then find your way.

  • Walk the map with the direction buttons or your arrow keys.
  • Every building has one door (the small brown mark). To go in, walk along the street past the door, then choose Entra a la izquierda or Entra a la derecha.
  • Every playthrough has new errands, new routes and new clues.
  • Turn on Desafío: solo audio to hide the text of every message.

    Para el profe: how this scene works
    • This scene is mostly interpretation: students listen to or read a message, then act on it. The only production is choosing the right question to ask a passerby.
    • Directions are relative to the way the student is facing, so dobla a la derecha has to be understood, not matched to a compass. The dotted trail shows the route they actually took.
    • Each playthrough is different: Abuela Rosa’s errands, both walking routes and their directions, the bus times, the way Lucía describes her stop, and Lucía’s outfit and spot in the park are all generated fresh.
    • Each building has one door. Students enter by walking the block with the door, then choosing left or right. Standing beside the right building on the wrong street gets a pointer to the door’s street, not a penalty.
    • Wrong buildings get a reading response (Esto es el banco. Buscas la panadería.). After two wrong tries the destination glows on the map. Hints, mistakes and text reveals are counted, and each mission earns up to three stars.
    • Desafío: solo audio hides message text until the student taps Ver texto, which is counted. Nombres en el mapa off leaves only icons, so students must know panadería, farmacia, biblioteca.
    • Vocabulary: places in the community, transportation, telling time (las cinco menos cuarto), clothing and colours, and location words (al lado de, enfrente de, detrás de).
    • Outcomes practised: A–3.1 c (respond to simple instructions), LC–2.1 a (aural interpretation), LC–3.1 a (written interpretation), LC–3.3 a (meaning from visuals: map and schedule), LC–1.3 a (community, time, clothing). The Plaza de los Mariachis and Mercado San Juan are real Guadalajara landmarks (GC–1.2).
    • Audio uses the browser's built-in voices. In Edge on Windows, Mexican Spanish voices are available. Nothing is saved or sent anywhere.
    • Audio uses one Spanish voice from this computer: checking…. Microsoft Edge usually offers natural-sounding Microsoft voices. If it says “none found”, the audio buttons are hidden and the scene works with text only.
