    Para el profe: how this scene works
    • Each trip picks a month (enero or julio) and three of eight cities, with at least one hot and one cold stop. The weather is based on each city's real climate, so students meet hemisphere seasons: July is winter in Buenos Aires, Santiago and Cusco.
    • The forecast uses hace calor/frío/fresco/sol/viento, llueve, va a llover, está nublado, nieva and temperatures in words. Lucía's postcard adds an activity (beach, fancy dinner, mountain hike, museum) that may need one more item.
    • The suitcase holds six items. Missing items get a weather-based hint first (Va a llover. ¿Qué necesitas?) and a specific one on the second try. Packing a coat for 32 degrees is accepted but flagged.
    • Students then write a Llevo… sentence. The checker looks for llevo, items that are actually in the suitcase, and article agreement (una abrigo → un abrigo). Adding porque hace… earns a bonus.
    • Every piece of clothing comes in a colour (11 colours, changing each city). Students’ Llevo… sentences are checked for the right colour and for agreement (bufanda rojo → roja, zapatos negra → negros).
    • El vestidor (after the three cities): four rounds where Lucía or Diego describes an outfit for an occasion (Hoy llueve y voy al centro. Quiero llevar una chaqueta amarilla, unos pantalones grises…). Students drag pieces onto a paper doll; each requested piece also appears in a second colour as a decoy. Feedback names the mismatch (La falda es azul. Lucía quiere una falda amarilla.).
    • Outcomes practised: LC–1.3 a (clothing, weather, geography), LC–2.1 a, LC–3.1 a, LC–3.2 a, A–4.2 a (gather information), GC–1.2 b and GC–2.4 b (geography and climate shape life in a region).
    • Audio uses one Spanish voice from this computer: checking…. Microsoft Edge usually offers natural-sounding Microsoft voices. If it says “none found”, the audio buttons are hidden and the scene works with text only.
