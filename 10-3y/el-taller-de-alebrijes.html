    Para el profe: how this scene works
    • Simón dice (eight commands): Tócate la nariz, Señala las rodillas, Levanta los brazos, Mueve los hombros… across fourteen body parts. Two commands leave out Simón dice, and the student should press ¡No! instead. A wrong click names the part they clicked (Eso es el cuello), so mistakes still teach vocabulary.
    • Pedidos: three customers describe an alebrije: number of heads, eyes, ears (grandes/pequeñas), horns, arms, legs, wings, tail (larga/corta) and dots, with colours. The first order is a shelf of four similar alebrijes where only one fits. The next two are built part by part. Colour swatches have no names, so students have to know their colours. Feedback names each mismatch (El cliente quiere tres ojos. Tu alebrije tiene dos.).
    • Tu alebrije: students build any creature, then describe it. The checker compares the text with the build: numbers (un ojo, una cola, cuatro brazos), colour agreement (alas rojas, puntos amarillos, un alebrije morado), size agreement (orejas pequeñas, cola larga) and negatives (No tiene alas). It needs five correct details and no wrong ones. Se llama… puts a name plate on the creature.
    • Culture: alebrijes began with Pedro Linares, a cartonero (papier-mâché artisan) in Mexico City. Today, villages near Oaxaca such as San Martín Tilcajete and San Antonio Arrazola carve them from copal wood and paint them with bright patterns.
    • Outcomes practised: A–3.1 c (respond to simple commands), A–5.1 a and A–5.2 a (use the language for fun and creatively), A–1.1 a–b, LC–1.3 a (the body, people and description, colours and numbers), LC–2.1 a, LC–3.1 a, LC–3.2 a, LC–3.3 a.
