Abuela Rosa:
Para el profe: how this scene works
  • Requests arrive in four forms, chosen at random: Tienes que lavar los platos, Lava los platos, por favor, ¿Puedes lavar los platos? and Hay que lavar los platos. Students must connect each command form (including pon, haz, riega, dale) to the infinitive they choose from.
  • Requests arrive with infinitives or commands, but students answer “¿Qué haces?” in the yo form (lavo, pongo, hago, riego, le doy de comer), so they conjugate in their heads. Each object also offers one tú-form distractor (lavas los platos), which gets a targeted correction.
  • Several objects have more than one real chore (lavo / ordeno la ropa, lavo / seco los platos, pongo / limpio la mesa, le doy de comer / baño al perro), so the verb matters, not just the noun.
  • Twelve jobs win the round. Each expired request costs a heart; three lost hearts end it early. Modo tranquilo removes the clock for students who need more time. Desafío: solo audio hides request text until Ver texto, which is counted.
  • The round ends with a short written note: one sentence with voy a + infinitive and one with tener que + infinitive. A conjugated verb after voy a (voy a lavo) gets a targeted correction.
  • Outcomes practised: A–3.1 c (respond to simple instructions), A–3.2 (state personal actions), LC–1.3 a (activities in the home), LC–1.4 b (tener que, ir a + infinitive), LC–2.1 a, LC–3.1 a, LC–3.2 a.
  • Audio uses one Spanish voice from this computer: checking…. Microsoft Edge usually offers natural-sounding Microsoft voices. If it says “none found”, the audio buttons are hidden and the scene works with text only.
