      Para el profe: how this scene works
      • Every game picks a new culprit and new family details (hair, glasses, shirt colour, room, favourite cake flavour). The three clues always point to exactly one person, and the two clues students can see never narrow it down to one on their own. They have to ask about flavours to solve it.
      • Questions are typed. The parser understands the common 10-3Y frames: ¿Cómo te llamas?, ¿Cuántos años tienes?, ¿Te gusta el chocolate?, ¿Cuál es tu sabor favorito?, ¿Dónde estás?, ¿Dónde está Tomás?, ¿Tienes hambre?, ¿Tienes lentes? Unrecognised questions get “¿Cómo? No entiendo” and are counted.
      • The culprit lies about their own favourite flavour. Self-reports alone never solve the case: students have to notice the contradiction and check with other family members, using third-person questions (¿Cuál es el sabor favorito de Beto? ¿A tu papá le gusta el chocolate?). Everyone else tells the truth about everyone. Hunger and direct “did you eat it?” questions give no tells. Writing mintió or miente in the accusation earns the Detector de mentiras badge.
      • The three clues are objects in the house: the parrot’s cage in the sala, the empty plate on the dining table, and a recipe stuck to the fridge. Finding the plate lays a trail of cake crumbs from the table to the culprit’s room (up the ladder for upstairs rooms), so students can see what Las migas van hacia el dormitorio means.
      • The Preguntas útiles chips insert a question frame for students who need a scaffold. Missing accents and a missing ¿ are pointed out after each question.
      • A wrong accusation gets an in-character alibi tied to the clue that rules that person out (¡Mi camiseta es azul!). Naming two or more clues in the accusation earns the Con pruebas badge.
      • Vocabulary: family members, describing people (pelo, lentes, colours), rooms of the house, foods and flavours, gustar, tener, estar for location. Se comió is used as a set phrase.
      • Outcomes practised: A–1.1 b (ask for and provide basic information), A–4.3 a–b (problem solving), LC–1.3 a (people around me, home), LC–3.1 a and LC–3.2 a, LC–5.3 a (question–answer patterns).
      • Audio uses one Spanish voice from this computer: checking…. Microsoft Edge usually offers natural-sounding Microsoft voices. If it says “none found”, the audio buttons are hidden and the scene works with text only.
