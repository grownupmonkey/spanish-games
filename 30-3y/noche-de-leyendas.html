        Para el profe: how this game works
        • Shape of a run (25–35 min): Lucía’s welcome → El tuk-tuk (always first: say the posada, catch the price, bargain) → three of six city scenes in random order → Noche de leyendas with Don Chepe. Between scenes, a map of Antigua: Lucía’s voice message, the next pin, two optional side encounters and hidden ✦ Datos de Antigua.
        • City scenes: El comedor (order from three daily specials, one sold out; she brings the wrong drink; add up the bill) · El mercado de artesanías (buy the gift Lucía described in her voice note — item, colour, budget — and bargain into budget) · La policía de turismo (ask for something politely, then follow spoken directions on the map) · La terminal de camionetas (find the right bus by listening to three ayudantes; ask tomorrow’s time and fare) · Algo se perdió (say what you lost; tell what you did this afternoon in the preterite; describe it in the imperfect; follow directions to where it was found) · El café (order; catch the wrong change).
        • Legends: nearly every local tells a short piece of a legend (El Sombrerón, La Tatuana, El Cadejo, La Llorona, Doña Beatriz y el Volcán de Agua, La Siguanaba), followed by one listening question. Four of six are heard per run; the finale retells one of them in full. Students rebuild the order from audio cards, answer a question about a character’s motives, write the ending (≥ 4 past verbs, ≥ 2 preterite, ≥ 15 words), then hear the real one. Don Chepe reacts to ideas that match.
        • Checked in student writing: preterite (regular, spelling-change yo forms llegué, busqué, empecé, stem-changing pidió, durmió, irregular tuvo, hizo, dijo, fue), imperfect for description, present tense where the past is needed (corre → corrió), invented forms (hació → hizo, teneó → tuvo), yo vs. él (yo fue → fui), and the personal a (vio la muchacha → vio a la muchacha). Accents never fail an answer; they get tips. Usted is suggested with adult strangers; the barista uses tú. Modelled, never marked: future (saldrá, le costará), conditional (¿me podría…?), subjunctive (cuando llegue a la esquina, quiero que le compres…, que no cueste más de…).
        • Audio-only by default. ⚙ Ajustes turns on text (subtitles, option text, card text). The accommodation link adds ?texto=1 to the URL. If a computer has no Spanish voice, text turns on automatically. The game records whether text was used and why, for the report later. Replay and speed are always available.
        • Chapín Spanish: patojo, pisto, chucho, ¡va pues!, ¡púchica!, cabal, fíjese que…, cruce a la derecha, ¿me lo deja en…?, and a young barista who slips into vos (tenés razón… digo, tienes razón).
        • What is randomized: the three city scenes and their order; every character (from cast pools); posada, menu, sold-out dish, wrong drink, prices; the gift, colour and budget; the bus destinations, times and fares; the lost item and where it turns up; every map route (start, facing, turns, distances, decoys); which legend each local tells and which one the finale uses; side encounters; which Datos are hidden; and line variants within each scene.
        • Outcomes practised (30-3Y): LC–1.3 a (folk tales, legends and fables; plus restaurants, shopping and transport revisited) · LC–1.4 b (preterite, imperfect, personal a) · LC–1.4 a (preterite vs. imperfect, conditional, subjunctive, modelled) · A–1.1 a · A–3.2 a (state personal actions in the past) · A–4.2 b (story order) · A–4.3 a, b (describe a problem; solutions) · A–4.4 a (characters and their motivations in a story) · A–5.2 a · LC–2.1 a · LC–2.3 a · LC–4.1 b · LC–4.3 a · LC–5.1 a (time sequencing), c (directions) · LC–5.2 a (folk tales) · GC–1.2 a · GC–2.2 a · GC–3.1 a.
