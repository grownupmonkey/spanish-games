        Para el profe: how this scene works
        • Las compras: Abuela Chayo gives the student a list of 4–5 ingredients for one dish (enfrijoladas, tlayudas, chiles de agua rellenos or salsa y guacamole) and a budget. Each listed item sits in two baskets at different distances with different prices, and prices are only given when asked (¿Cuánto cuestan aquellos aguacates?), in words. To buy, the student names a quantity (un kilo, medio kilo, un cuarto de kilo, una docena, un manojo, tres aguacates) and points with a demonstrative: front row near the student = este, counter near the vendor = ese, back shelf far from both = aquel. If they don’t point, the vendor asks ¿Cuáles?. Agreement (estas jitomates → estos jitomates) and distance mistakes are explained. The budget only works if the student asks prices and mostly picks the cheaper basket; haggling (¿Me lo deja en…?) earns a small discount once per stall. Going over budget never blocks the game.
        • La trampa del jitomate: in central and southern Mexico, jitomate is the red tomato and tomate (verde) is the tomatillo. Lists with jitomates put tomates verdes on the same stall.
        • Register: tú forms to vendors (dame, ¿me das?, ¿tienes?) get a note towards usted (deme, ¿me da?, ¿tiene?). At the fonda, Abuela Chayo and Don Manuel get usted and nine-year-old Tomás gets tú.
        • La fonda: each family member has two randomized food preferences (vegetarian, loves meat, no spicy food, loves spicy food, no beans, no cheese, loves cheese), so only one or two dishes on the board fit. The student asks (¿Le gusta el picante? ¿Come carne? ¿Qué le gusta comer? ¿Te gustan los frijoles?); answers appear as notes in third person (A la abuela no le gusta el picante). The menu board lists each dish’s ingredients and whether it is spicy. The student orders for everyone through the waitress (Para la abuela, la sopa de guías…); a wrong dish gets a complaint and must be changed. Gustar agreement in questions is checked (¿Le gusta los frijoles? → ¿Le gustan…?, usted gusta → le gusta).
        • ¿Y a ti? The student writes about food: at least three gustar/encantar sentences, including one about themselves and one about someone else (A mi papá le encanta…, A mis amigos les gustan…). The checker corrects number (me gusta los tacos → me gustan, me gustan bailar → me gusta), mi gusta → me gusta, yo gusto → me gusta and le/les (a mis padres le gusta → les gusta).
        • Hidden gems: six Datos de Oaxaca: chapulines, quesillo, los siete moles, el pasillo de humo, the chocolate mills and tejate (with a note on Oaxaca’s Indigenous languages). Mexican market language in the dialogue: ¡Pásele, pásele!, ¿Qué le damos?, marchante, ahí le va, ¿algo más?, ándele, ¿mande?, se lo dejo en…, fiado.
