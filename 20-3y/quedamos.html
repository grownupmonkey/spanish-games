    Para el profe: how this scene works
    • ¿Quién puede? The student is on an exchange week with Camila’s family and messages four of her friends. Friends answer what they are asked: availability (El sábado por la mañana tengo que trabajar en la panadería de mi tío, pero por la tarde estoy libre) and tastes (Me encanta ir a la playa, pero no sé nadar…). The student fills in a Saturday/Sunday agenda from what they read; a fully correct agenda earns a badge.
    • El plan: the student proposes a plan in the group chat (what, which day, morning/afternoon/evening). Each friend accepts or declines politely with a reason. Every weekend has one or two plans that work for all four, and several near misses. Singular forms in the group (¿Quieres…?) get a gentle note towards ustedes (¿Quieren…? ¿Pueden…? ¿Les gusta…?); plural forms in a private chat get the opposite note.
    • El permiso: the student asks Doña Maritza for permission. Tú forms addressed to her (¿me dejas?, ¿cómo estás?, tu hija) get a register note; usted forms (¿me da permiso?, ¿cómo está?, su hija) are praised. She uses tú with the teenager, which the notes point out. She asks who they’re going with and when they’ll be back, and negotiates a curfew.
    • Cambio de planes: one friend cancels; the reply is checked for a kind response (¡Qué pena! No te preocupes. ¡Otro día!). Then a classmate invites the student to something at the same time. A polite decline needs an apology or thanks, no puedo and a reason (tengo que…, ya tengo planes, voy a…); suggesting another day is a bonus. Accepting by mistake means breaking the engagement politely afterwards.
    • Randomized each playthrough: which four friends, their busy times and reasons, what each likes and dislikes (some can’t swim or dance: no sé nadar, no sé bailar), who cancels, the classmate’s invitation and the curfew.
    • Hidden gems: six Datos de San Juan: El Morro and its chiringas (kites), la garita, el coquí, la piragua, the blue adoquines and la bomba. Puerto Rican usage in the dialogue: pana, janguear, ¡wepa!, brutal, ¡ay, bendito!, chiringa.
    • Outcomes practised (20-3Y): A–6.1 a–c (apologize and refuse politely, show interest, make and break social engagements) · A–3.2 a–c (wishes, invitations, ability: sé / no sé) · A–3.1 b (seek permission) · A–1.1 a · LC–1.3 a (social life) · LC–1.4 b (querer, poder, venir, salir, jugar, saber; tener que; ir a; me/te/les gusta) · LC–3.1 a · LC–3.2 a · LC–4.1 a–b (tú, usted, ustedes) · LC–5.3 a–b (opening and closing, invitation and acceptance or refusal) · GC–2.2 a (different words for the same thing).
