      Para el profe: how this scene works
      • El calentamiento: the coach’s whiteboard shows five exercises in pictures, drawn at random from ten (run, walk, jump, arms up, stretch, touch your toes, turn around, sit down, kick the ball, drink water). Students give affirmative ustedes commands in order (Corran. Levanten los brazos. Estírense.) and the team does them. A tú or vos command makes just one player do it (¿Yo sola?), which shows why the group needs ustedes. Usted forms (corra), statements (corren) and infinitives (correr) are corrected; missing accents on estírense, siéntense, tóquense are noted. The Argentine elonguen (stretch) is accepted.
      • La radio (unguided listening): a random first half called in the present progressive by a radio commentator (Cami le está pasando la pelota a Martina… ¡está pateando!). Then Don Beto, who was busy at the grill, asks four questions: the score, who scored, who got the yellow card or who made the save, and what the players are doing now. Answers can be short (names, numbers); progressive answers are checked. With the audio-only challenge on, the commentary is hidden until revealed.
      • El relato: three animated plays on the pitch. Students call each one in writing with the present progressive, naming the player: at least two actions per play (¡Martina está corriendo! ¡Le está pasando la pelota a Juli! ¡Juli está pateando… gol!). Checked: estar agreement (las chicas está → están), gerund forms (correndo → corriendo, pasiendo → pasando, cayendo, pidiendo), estar + infinitive (está correr → está corriendo), ser for estar (es corriendo), and the simple present where the progressive is expected. After three tries the commentator models the call.
      • La entrevista: students ask the star at least three questions with tú or vos (¿Desde cuándo juegas? ¿Qué otros deportes practicas? ¿Cuántas veces entrenas?). Checked: jugar (jugas → juegas / jugás, juegar → jugar, jugo is juice → juego) and register. Her answers model hace + time, the preterite and ir a + infinitive.
      • Hidden gems: six Datos de Buenos Aires: the Obelisco, the hinchada and its drums, fileteado porteño, choripán, mate and the bandoneón (tango). Argentine Spanish: vos (sos, tenés, jugás, mirá), che, re, genial, buenísimo, pibe/piba, gambeta, arquera.
