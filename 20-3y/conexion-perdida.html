Conexión perdida

Every playthrough brings a different bag, flights and announcements.

  • La maleta perdida: describe your bag at the lost-luggage desk (Es una maleta roja y grande, con una cinta amarilla) until the agent finds it.
  • ¡Cancelado!: ask about the new flights (¿A qué hora llega? ¿Cuánto cuesta? ¿Es directo?), choose the right one for Lucía’s plans, and tell her what you’re going to do (Voy a tomar el vuelo de las 15:40).
  • La puerta: listen to the announcements, help an older lady with the departures board, and find your gate.
  • La postal: write home about your plans in Cusco with ir a + infinitive.
  • Airport staff and older people: usted.

    Para el profe: how this scene works
    • La maleta perdida: the student’s bag (a maleta, bolso or mochila, with a colour, a size and one special detail) is shown as a photo. The agent’s screen shows six lookalike bags; each matching detail the student gives (Es una maleta roja. Es grande. Tiene una cinta amarilla.) rules out the others, so every detail counts. Details that don’t match the photo are questioned. Checked: adjective and article agreement with the bag (una maleta rojo → roja, un mochila → una mochila, un bolso pequeña → pequeño) and usted with the agent. Then: where are you going to stay? (from Lucía’s message).
    • ¡Cancelado!: one of three situations from Lucía (you must arrive today; arrive tomorrow but don’t spend money; arrive before 22:00). The agent offers three flights; students ask about arrival time, price, stops and seats (¿A qué hora llega el de las 15:40? ¿Cuánto cuesta? ¿Es directo? ¿Hay asientos?); answers fill a table. They choose (Voy a tomar el vuelo de las 15:40) and text Lucía their plan with ir a + infinitive; if the choice doesn’t fit her plans, she says why and they go back to the counter.
    • La puerta (unguided listening): airport announcements in Spanish with other flights mixed in. Doña Ermelinda asks when her flight leaves and from which gate; students read the departures monitor and answer (Va a salir a las 16:20 de la puerta 7.; times in digits or words). Then a gate change is announced and students click the right gate.
    • La postal: at least three plans with ir a + infinitive (Voy a visitar Machu Picchu. Lucía y yo vamos a tomar el tren.), with greeting and closing for full marks. Checked throughout: voy a visito → visitar, voy visitar → voy a visitar, the form of ir with the subject (Lucía y yo va → vamos).
    • Hidden gems: six Datos del Perú: Machu Picchu, the Nazca lines, Inca Kola, ceviche, words Spanish took from Quechua (papa, cancha, choclo, llama, cóndor) and the Andean condor. Peruvian Spanish: ¡ya!, al toque, chévere, joven.
    • Outcomes practised (20-3Y): LC–1.3 a (vacations and travel) · LC–1.4 b (ir a + infinitive; tener que; querer, poder) · LC–1.4 a (preterite, modelled) · LC–1.4 c (adjective agreement) · A–4.3 a, b (recognize and describe a problem, then propose solutions) · A–1.1 a, b (ask for and provide information; describe things) · A–3.1 a (requests) · A–3.2 a (express a wish to do something) · A–2.1 b · LC–2.1 a (the announcements) · LC–2.3 a · LC–3.1 a (the departures board) · LC–3.2 a (the postcard) · LC–4.1 b · LC–4.3 a · GC–1.2 b · GC–1.4 a · GC–2.2 a.
