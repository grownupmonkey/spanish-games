Mismo idioma, otro mundo

Every playthrough brings five different callers in a different order.

  • ¿De dónde eres?: ask each caller questions (¿Qué comes en el desayuno? ¿Qué tiempo hace? ¿Cómo dices «cool»? ¿Cómo es tu ciudad?), listen for their words and customs, then guess: click a city on the map or say Eres de…
  • Then they ask about Lethbridge: answer with a full sentence (En Lethbridge hace mucho viento. Yo prefiero el hockey.).
  • Mi mundo hispano: write a post comparing two of their cities with Lethbridge (En Bogotá comen arepas, pero aquí nadie come arepas en el desayuno.).
  • They’re teenagers: tú (or vos, vosotros…).
  • Click the sparkles for Datos del mundo hispano.

    Para el profe: how this scene works
    • ¿De dónde eres? (five calls): five of the seven friends from the trip (Bogotá, San Juan, Oaxaca, Madrid, Buenos Aires, Puerto Viejo, Lima) call with blurred backgrounds. Each greets in their own way (¡Quiubo, parce! · ¡Wepa! · ¡Qué onda! · ¿Qué tal, tío? · ¿Cómo andás? · ¡Pura vida, mae! · ¡Hola, causa!). Students ask about food, weather, words, their city and free time; the clues fill a notebook. At least one question before guessing; wrong guesses get an extra clue. The background is revealed after the right guess.
    • Lethbridge: after each guess the caller asks a question about Lethbridge (food, weather, free time, the city). Students answer with a sentence; the callers react, modelling también, tampoco, nadie, algo.
    • Checked (20-3Y structured): tú with teen callers (¿Conoce…? → ¿Conoces…?); irregular present (preferes → prefieres, prefero → prefiero, conoco → conozco, sabo → sé, poneo → pongo, traio → traigo); pronouns after prepositions (para yo → para mí, a yo → a mí, para tú → para ti, con tú → contigo, con yo → conmigo); gustar agreement. Comparatives (más… que) are modelled at 30-3Y, so they’re accepted but not required.
    • Mi mundo hispano: a post with at least two facts about two different cities (checked against what the callers said, so En Madrid comen arepas is questioned), a sentence about Lethbridge and a connector (también, tampoco, pero, en cambio, nadie, alguien).
    • Hidden gems: six Datos del mundo hispano: Spanish around the world, Equatorial Guinea, the letter ñ, the Día del Idioma (23 April), English words from Spanish (rodeo, canyon, ranch…) and the Lethbridge Viaduct.
    • Outcomes practised (20-3Y): LC–1.3 a (cultural diversity; daily life in the Spanish-speaking world) · LC–1.4 b (irregular present: conocer, saber, preferir, poner, traer; pronouns after prepositions; alguien, nadie, algo, nada, también; gustar) · LC–1.4 c · A–1.1 a, b · A–2.1 a, b · A–4.1 a (personal observations) · A–4.2 b (compare and contrast in simple ways) · A–6.1 b (talk about themselves and respond with interest) · LC–2.1 a · LC–2.3 a · LC–3.2 a · LC–4.1 b · LC–4.2 a · LC–4.3 a (regional variation) · GC–1.2 b · GC–1.4 a · GC–2.1 a · GC–2.2 a, b (different words for the same thing; regional differences) · GC–2.3 a, b (connections with their own culture) · GC–2.4 a.
    • Audio uses one Spanish voice from this computer: checking…. If it says “none found”, the audio buttons are hidden and the scene works with text only.
