¿Moda o no moda?

Every playthrough brings different customers, clothes and opinions.

  • El probador: customers come out of the fitting room and ask what you think. Be honest (Esa chaqueta te queda grande) and suggest something better (Pruébate una talla más pequeña). Older customers: usted.
  • ¿De quién es?: give a group of friends back their things with possessives (Hugo, ¿es tu gorra? ¿Son tus zapatillas? ¿Es vuestra esta bolsa?).
  • La encuesta: ask people in the park what they think of the latest trends (¿Qué te parecen las riñoneras?), then write a post with the results and your opinion.
  • In Spain people say vosotros to a group of friends. You’ll hear it; you can use ustedes too.

      Para el profe: how this scene works
      • El probador: three customers per playthrough (at least one older customer, who gets usted; Doña Carmen explains that Spaniards use tú a lot, but usted with older people). Each comes out in something that doesn’t work: a top that’s too big, too small or clashes with the trousers, trousers that are too long or too short, shoes that are too big or too small. Students give an honest opinion (Creo que esa chaqueta te queda grande. Los pantalones le quedan largos.), then suggest a fix (Pruébate una talla más pequeña. ¿Por qué no se prueba otro color? Doña Carmen te los puede cortar.). The customer comes back in a better fit and needs a positive opinion.
      • Checked: quedar agreement (los zapatos te queda → quedan), adjective agreement (la chaqueta te queda pequeño → pequeña), demonstrative agreement, register (te / le, pruébate / pruébese). The clothes the customer is wearing are near the listener, so ese / esa is noted when students say este / esta (not marked wrong).
      • ¿De quién es?: six mixed-up items on the fitting-room bench, five belonging to four friends (each friend has a colour) and one belonging to all of them. A general question (¿De quién es esta gorra?) gets a clue. Students return each item by asking its owner with a possessive (Hugo, ¿es tu gorra? Alba, ¿son tus zapatillas? Chicos, ¿es vuestra esta bolsa?). Checked: tu / tus, vuestro / vuestra, tuyo / tuya agreement, es / son, su with a friend, mi for tu. Su or de ustedes to the group is accepted.
      • La encuesta: four people in the Retiro (one older, usted) give opinions on three random trends (riñoneras, calcetines con sandalias, ropa de segunda mano…). Students ask (¿Qué te parecen los vaqueros rotos? ¿Le gusta el chándal?); parecer / gustar agreement and register are checked. Then a short Instagram post: at least two results (A Iker le encantan las riñoneras. A Don Ramón no le gustan los vaqueros rotos.), checked against the poll, and their own opinion (Creo que la ropa de segunda mano mola porque es barata.).
      • Hidden gems: six Datos de Madrid: el Rastro, the tiled street signs, gatos (madrileños), the bear and the strawberry tree, chocolate con churros and the Palacio de Cristal. Spain’s Spanish: vosotros (¿Habéis visto…? ¿Qué queréis?), guay, mola, vale, tío / tía, qué chulo, hortera, bocata.
      • Outcomes practised (20-3Y): LC–1.3 a (shopping, fashion, fads) · LC–1.4 b (demonstratives; possessives; gustar, parecer, quedar; tú commands, including reflexive) · LC–1.4 a (perfect tense, modelled) · LC–1.4 c (adjective agreement) · A–2.1 a, b (express a personal response; inquire about and express preferences) · A–1.1 a, b (describe things) · A–3.1 a, d (requests; encourage or discourage a course of action) · LC–2.1 a · LC–2.3 a · LC–3.2 a · LC–3.3 a · LC–4.1 b · LC–4.2 a · LC–4.3 a (vosotros, Spain’s slang) · GC–1.2 b · GC–1.4 a · GC–2.2 a.
