        Para el profe: how this game works
        • Shape of a run (25–35 min): Lucía’s welcome → El ómnibus (always first: say where you are going, catch the fare, say how long you have been in Montevideo with hace) → three of six scenes in random order → La merienda at the house of a friend of Lucía’s grandparents. Between scenes, a map of Montevideo: Lucía’s voice message, the next pin, two optional side encounters and hidden ✦ Datos de Montevideo.
        • Scenes: La rayuela (a grandmother tells how she played; tap the squares in the order you hear, including skipping one and going back) · Las escondidas (a grandfather’s memory; clues with detrás, a la derecha, a la izquierda, entre tell you where the child is hiding; ask him about his games) · Las figuritas (swap duplicates for the sticker Lucía’s cousin needs; bargain on counts) · La merienda (order at the confitería, one item sold out, wrong drink, add up the bill in pesos) · Las llamadas (candombe drummer; ask ¿hace cuánto que toca?) · El delantal blanco (a retired teacher describes her school; write about yours).
        • Memories: nearly every local shares a short memory in the imperfect (mate with the grandfather, Sundays on the Rambla, August kites, the match on the radio, murgas, rainy afternoons), each followed by one listening question. The finale host (Don Washington, Doña Nelly or Don Hugo) tells a whole childhood day; students order five events, answer a question about why, then write about their own childhood (≥ 3 imperfect verbs, ≥ 1 hace + time, ≥ 20 words). The host reacts to ideas that match (games, food, family, school).
        • Checked in student writing: the imperfect (jugaba, era, tenía, vivíamos, íbamos), hace + a period of time, and the same invented-form and personal a tips as game 1. Accents never fail an answer; they get tips. Usted is suggested with older strangers. Present-tense answers get a nudge toward the imperfect.
        • Audio-only by default. ⚙ Ajustes turns on text (subtitles, option text, card text). The accommodation link adds ?texto=1 to the URL. If a computer has no Spanish voice, text turns on automatically. The game records whether text was used and why, for the report later. Replay and speed are always available.
        • Uruguayan Spanish: gurí, botija, ta, bo, pila, cabal, plus a few vos slips from younger characters (¿querés…? … digo, ¿quiere…?). Students are only expected to use tú and usted.
        • What is randomized: the three scenes and their order; every character (from cast pools); destination, fare, menu, sold-out item, wrong drink, prices, sticker and counts; hopscotch sequences, hide-and-seek objects and clues; years and class sizes; which memory each local shares; which host the finale uses; side encounters; which Datos are hidden; and line variants within each scene.
        • Outcomes practised (30-3Y): memories and games of childhood · imperfect and hace + time · describing habits and past experiences · sequencing a story · spatial words · cultural comparison. Check the exact outcome codes against the POS before using this for assessment.
