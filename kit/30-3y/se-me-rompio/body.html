Se me rompió

Lucía is in Medellín, Colombia. Today you help Fabio at Reparaciones El Parcero, a phone-repair shop. Three customers come in with broken phones.

  • La foto. Each customer shows you a picture of what happened. Find and label the things in it, then sort the words.
  • La historia. Listen to the customer’s story while the picture comes to life.
  • La conversación. Examine the phone, then rebuild the conversation like a text chat: drag each message to the left (the customer) or the right (the technician), in the right order.
  • El informe. At the end, write the day’s report using your word bank.

The stories are spoken; picture labels and chat messages are written. You can turn on text for the conversations in ⚙ Ajustes, and your report will say so. Click the sparkles in the shop to find six Datos de Medellín.

        Para el profe
        • How it works. Three customers, each in five steps, then a written report. (1) Picture study (PWIM): a detailed illustrated scene of how the phone broke; students click to find eight things (people and things, places, actions), hearing each word and seeing its label; every word goes into a word bank. (2) Sorting: students classify the eight words into people and things / places / actions. (3) The story (Comprehensible Input): the customer narrates in six short preterite sentences while the picture animates each event (MovieTalk-style). (4) Examine the phone: a close-up with the damaged part labelled. (5) The conversation: thirteen messages from the counter conversation (the problem, part of the story, the technician’s usted questions and the customer’s answers, the repair with impersonal se and lo). Students drag each message into a phone-style chat, left for the customer and right for the technician, in order. Bubbles take the speaker’s colour as they are placed; after Comprobar, correct ones lock with a tick and wrong ones glow red. After the third check the correct conversation is shown. Once it is right, the whole conversation is read aloud. Finally, the day’s report, written from the word bank (the PWIM step from words to sentences and paragraphs).
        • Randomized every time. Three of six cases (Metrocable, a rainstorm, the dog, a tinto in a café, the ciclovía, a hot car), three of six customers, the day it happened and when the phone was bought.
        • Grammar. Modelled and recognized in the conversation: preterite questions and answers with usted (¿Cuándo pasó? ¿Qué hizo después? ¿Cuándo lo compró?), impersonal se and direct object pronouns in the repair (Se cambia la pantalla. Lo tiene listo mañana.). Produced and checked in the day’s report: five or more preterite verbs with the series’ corrections for irregular and spelling-change forms, se at least twice (se le cayó, se rompió, se cambió), with agreement checked for impersonal se (se cambia la pantalla / se cambian las piezas).
        • Modelled, never marked. Accidental se in every story (se me cayó, se me mojó, se me dañó), the imperfect in descriptions (no tenía paraguas, estaba muy caliente), Colombian vocabulary (celular, prender, parlante, paradero, parqueadero, tinto, parce).
        • Text. The stories are audio-only by default; picture labels and chat messages are always written (each message also has ▶ to hear it). ?texto=1 turns text on for the spoken parts; the report notes when text was used.
        • Stars. Each customer: 3★ with at most one slip (more than two sorting errors counts as one; each extra Comprobar in the conversation counts as one, up to two). Report: 5+ preterite verbs, se twice, 6+ word-bank words, two customers named (3★ with at most one slip).
        • Accuracy notes. The shop, Fabio and the customers are invented. Medellín facts: the Metrocable (2004) was the first cable car built as everyday public transport; Comuna 13’s outdoor escalators and murals; the City of Eternal Spring (about 22 °C); the Feria de las Flores and silleteros in August; a tinto is a small black coffee; ¿Qué más, parce? is a paisa greeting. Prices on the café menu are in Colombian pesos.
        • 2D graphics. PixiJS (loaded from cdnjs, with jsDelivr as a backup). Light on older machines; no graphics card needed.
