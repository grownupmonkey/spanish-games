Se busca

Lucía is in Barcelona, and so are you: you want a summer job! Everything happens on Curro, a job app on your phone.

  • Las ofertas. Read six job postings. The app asks questions out loud; tap the posting that answers each one.
  • Los candidatos. Listen to four applicants’ voice messages and match each one to the right job. Then sort phrases: sé… or conozco…?
  • La entrevista. Choose a job and do a video interview. Answer about yourself: what you’ve done (he trabajado…), what you know (sé… / conozco…), last summer (trabajé…) and this summer (trabajaré…).
  • Tu perfil. Write the “Sobre mí” for your profile, and wait for a message…

Postings are written; questions, voice messages and the interview are spoken. You can turn on text for the spoken parts in ⚙ Ajustes, and your report will say so. Click the sparkles on the terrace to find six Datos de Barcelona.

        • How it works. Four missions on a phone job app (Curro, Spanish slang for “job”). (1) Las ofertas: six written postings (a summer camp, a lifeguard, a beach bar, a bike-tour guide, a souvenir shop, a market kitchen). The app asks five spoken questions (¿Para qué trabajo hay que saber nadar muy bien?); students tap the right posting. (2) Los candidatos: four applicants leave voice messages (five sentences each, with the perfect, preterite, future and saber/conocer); students drag each one onto the right job. Two postings are decoys. Then they sort eight phrases from the messages into Sé… or Conozco…. (3) La entrevista: students choose any posting and do a spoken video interview in usted. Four questions, answered about themselves: experience (perfect), skills and places (saber/conocer), last summer (preterite) and plans (simple future). If an answer misses the structure, the interviewer asks a follow-up that models it aloud. (4) Tu perfil: a “Sobre mí” of about 50 words, shown live on the phone, then a job offer arrives.
        • No model to copy. Interview questions are spoken, and answers are personal. Help gives patterns (he + participle, sé + infinitive) rather than sentences.
        • Randomized every time. Which four applicants (and so which two decoy postings), the five questions, the eight saber/conocer phrases and their order.
        • Grammar checked (LC–1.4 b, structured). Perfect tense (he trabajado, he cuidado; missing h a trabajado → ha trabajado; irregular participles hacido → hecho; agreement he trabajada → he trabajado). Preterite with the series’ corrections. Simple future (trabajaré, ahorraré, haré; voy a is accepted but nudged). Saber vs. conocer: sé + infinitive, sé que…; conozco + place/person, with personal a (conozco a Marta); mix-ups (conozco nadar → sé nadar, sé Barcelona → conozco Barcelona), forms (sabo → sé, conoco → conozco) and the accent on sé.
        • Modelled, never marked. The interviewer’s usted (cuénteme, ¿qué hará?), Spain’s vocabulary (canguro, zumo, chiringuito, instituto, vale), the present subjunctive in set phrases (en lo que sea), and the perfect for recent events, which is more common in Spain than in Latin America.
        • Text. Postings and the sorting phrases are written (reading tasks). Questions, voice messages and the interview are audio-only by default. ?texto=1 turns text on for students who need it; the report notes when text was used.
        • Stars. Ofertas: 3★ with at most one wrong tap. Candidatos: 3★ when all matches are right on the first check and at most one sorting error. Interview: 3★ with at most one slip (an answer missing its structure counts as one). Profile: 2+ perfect, 2+ preterite, 2+ future, sé and conozco both used correctly, about 50 words (3★ with at most one form slip).
        • Accuracy notes. The companies, people and postings are invented; pay is in euros per hour and plausible for summer work in Spain. Barcelona facts: Gaudí worked on the Sagrada Família for over forty years until his death in 1926 and is buried in its crypt; Catalan and Spanish are both official; the waterfront and beaches were rebuilt for the 1992 Olympics; La Boqueria market off La Rambla; Spain’s late lunch and dinner times; Sant Jordi (23 April) with books and roses.
